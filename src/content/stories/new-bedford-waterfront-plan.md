After a summer of public hearings, a plan to redraw how New Bedford uses its downtown waterfront is headed to the city council for a first vote next month.

The proposal pairs new public walkways and a small park with zoning changes meant to protect marine industrial uses along the working harbor, one of the most valuable commercial fishing ports in the country.

## What's in the plan

- A continuous harbor walk connecting the downtown historic district to the State Pier area.
- A marine industrial overlay that limits residential and hotel development on key parcels.
- New rules for how vacant waterfront lots can be used while they wait for redevelopment.

## What people are saying

Supporters at the hearings said the city has spent decades cut off from its own shoreline. Several fishing-industry speakers said they back public access in principle but want written assurances that loading, fuel and ice operations won't be squeezed out.

> "We're not against a walkway. We're against losing a berth," said a vessel owner at the August hearing.

## What happens next

The council is expected to refer the plan to committee before a final vote. Residents can submit written comments to the city clerk.
